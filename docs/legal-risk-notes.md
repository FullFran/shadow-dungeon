# Legal risk notes: Shadow Dungeon / Mazmorra Sombría

Status: research notes, compiled 2026-10-25. **This is not legal advice.** It is evidence gathering for a
risk decision about a free, ad-free, non-commercial browser Pokémon fan game (public repo
`FullFran/shadow-dungeon`, served at `https://www.fullfran.com/shadow-dungeon/`) that uses PMDCollab
SpriteCollab sprites (CC BY-NC 4.0), original procedural audio, and Pokémon species, move and item names,
and is built for a Twitch streamer (rociplays) to play live.

How to read this document:

- **[Fact]** means a primary source or reputable dated reporting supports it. The source is linked.
- **[Inference]** means it is my reading of the pattern. It is not established.
- **[Unverified]** means it is a claim I could not confirm from a primary or reputable source.

## 2. What the rights holders have said

- **[Fact]** Don McGowan, TPC chief legal officer from 2008 to 2020, in Aftermath (13 Mar 2024). Discovery came mostly from press: "someone from the company would send me a link to a news article". He also said "the worst thing on earth is when your 'fan' project gets press, because now I know about you". On crowdfunding: "You wait to see if they get funded (for a Kickstarter or similar); if they get funded then that's when you engage." And: "No one likes suing fans." https://aftermath.site/pokemon-lawyer-cease-desist-fan-project-pikachu-movie
- **[Fact]** The Nintendo of America notice to Game Jolt explicitly cited the host's advertising revenue. (Nintendo Life and Techdirt, Jan 2021, above.)
- **[Fact, reported]** Around AM2R, Nintendo said it must protect its IP and trademarks. The Prism letter said fan use "does not alter Nintendo's role to ensure its intellectual property is fully protected". (Bit-Tech, Game Informer.)

## 3. Patterns

### What correlates with takedowns ([Inference] unless marked)

1. **Visibility spikes and press coverage.** This is the strongest stated trigger. McGowan said so directly [Fact]. Uranium (1.5M downloads in about a week) and AM2R (anniversary release, Kotaku coverage) were acted on within days of going viral.
2. **Money anywhere in the chain.** That includes crowdfunding, which McGowan named [Fact], host ad revenue, which the Game Jolt notice named [Fact], and in-platform purchases (Brick Bronze, unverified).
3. **A "release event" for a full, finished-feeling game.** Examples are a launch date announced in advance (Prism) or a complete remake or replacement of an official title (AM2R, Uranium). Downloadable complete games are the archetype of a target.
4. **Timing near official releases or anniversaries.** Examples are AM2R (30th anniversary), Uranium (Pokémon GO summer) and Prism (Sun/Moon window). This is suggestive but not proven to be causal.
5. **Hubs and tooling that enable many games.** Examples are Essentials (2018), Game Jolt (2021) and Relic Castle (2024). TPC hit infrastructure, not just individual games.
6. **Platform hosts are the pressure point.** Most actions are DMCA notices to *hosts* (download mirrors, Game Jolt, Roblox, GitHub) rather than lawsuits against fans [Fact for the listed cases]. I found **no lawsuit against a non-commercial Pokémon fan game**. The only litigation found is Palworld, a commercial original-IP game, on patents.

### What long-lived projects do differently ([Inference])

- **Showdown, PokeMMO, PokéRogue, Infinite Fusion, Insurgence and PMDO** are all free. PokéRogue states "zero monetization" as a deliberate anti-DMCA policy.
- They tend to be **a tool, a niche or a different genre**, not a drop-in substitute for a current mainline game. Showdown is a battle simulator. PokéRogue is a roguelite. PMDO is a fan engine. PokeMMO requires the user's own ROMs.
- Several are **open source** or have **community-licensed assets** rather than ripped ones. Showdown and PokéRogue still use official names and sprites and survive anyway, so open source is not protective in itself.
- **Survivorship bias:** absence of action is not permission. Rights holders act selectively and without warning (Relic Castle had 20,000+ members and was hit in 2024 after years online).

## 4. Twitch and YouTube streaming

- **[Fact]** Nintendo's *Game Content Guidelines for Online Video & Image Sharing Platforms* (original 29 Nov 2018, updated 2 Sep 2024) cover "both uploads and livestreams". They allow monetization only through listed methods, which include the Twitch Affiliate and Twitch Partner programs and the YouTube Partner Program. https://www.nintendo.co.jp/networkservice_guideline/en/index.html
- **[Fact]** Those guidelines list as inappropriate content that "involves illegally copied or modified game software" and "game software produced using Nintendo's copyrighted material without Nintendo's authorization". The 2024 update added a right to object and to bar offenders from using Nintendo content. Siliconera https://siliconera.com/nintendo-updates-its-online-media-sharing-guidelines · Nintendo Life (Sep 2024) https://nintendolife.com/news/2024/09/nintendo-updates-content-creation-guidelines-to-clamp-down-on-multiplayer-meddling
- **[Inference]** So the guidelines **do not authorize** streaming fan games. If anything, fan games fall in the "produced without authorization" bucket. The guidelines are a permission grant for official games, not a safe harbour for fan games.
- **[Fact]** Nintendo and TPC do act on streams and videos in other contexts. Examples are DMCA strikes over leaked *Tears of the Kingdom* footage (Alanah Pearce, 2023: https://www.svg.com/1280780/zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-briefly-got-alanah-pearce-banned-twitch/), the GilvaSunner music channel (2022: https://archive.techdirt.com/articles/20220204/08354048414/gilvasunner-youtube-channel-shuts-down-due-to-copyright-strikes-nintendo-pokemon-releases-music.shtml) and Scarlet/Violet credits audio (2022, Ed Sheeran's label, not TPC).
- **[Fact, absence]** I found **no reported case** of Nintendo or TPC striking a Twitch stream for playing a Pokémon fan game. Uranium, Infinite Fusion and PokéRogue have been widely streamed.
- **[Inference]** The realistic stream risk is mostly **audio**. Ripped official music is the classic strike trigger, and Shadow Dungeon's audio is original and procedural, which removes that vector. A second risk is the game becoming visible because a channel or clip goes viral.

## 5. Our case: risk factors and reducers

### Current posture (from the brief; not re-audited here)

| Factor | Our status | Effect on risk [Inference] |
|---|---|---|
| Monetization | None (free, ad-free) | Lowers it. This removes the explicitly stated triggers. |
| Name | "Shadow Dungeon" / "Mazmorra Sombría", with no "Pokémon" in the title | Lowers it. It also avoids the trademark-in-title issue. |
| Sprites | SpriteCollab, CC BY-NC 4.0, credited | Mixed. The license is valid between contributors and us, but it **cannot license Nintendo/TPC's underlying character copyright**. The sprites depict Pokémon designs. |
| Audio | Original, procedural | Lowers it. This removes the most common strike vector. |
| Names (species, moves, items) | Uses Pokémon names | Raises it. These are trademarks and franchise text. Short names are weakly copyrightable, but trademark claims are possible. |
| Distribution | Browser, own domain, GitHub repo | Neutral to mixed. Browser play avoids "download a full game" optics like PokéRogue and Showdown. A public repo is a DMCA target, and notices there are **published** in `github/dmca`. |
| Visibility | Twitch streamer and viewers | Raises it. Press and virality are the stated discovery path. A small streamer is low; a viral clip is not. |
| Scope | Small roguelite / dungeon crawler | Lowers it. It is not a mainline substitute. |
| Disclaimer | Fan-project disclaimer on screen | Small effect. It shows good faith but has no legal force as a licence. |

### Recommendations now

1. **Keep zero money anywhere.** That means no donations, Ko-fi, Patreon or crowdfunding tied to the game, no ads on the game page, and no paid builds. Do not link the streamer's donation flow to the game page or repo. Streamer revenue from Twitch Affiliate is the streamer's own business, but avoid in-game "sub-only" or paid features.
2. **Never put "Pokémon" in the title, domain, repo name, logo or page `<title>`.** Use it only in the credits and disclaimer, nominatively ("fan project using Pokémon names").
3. **Do not use official logos, official fonts, UI trade dress, the Pokémon wordmark, ripped music or sound effects.** Keep audio original. Keep the SpriteCollab credits complete and respect its policy of not modifying Chunsoft sprites.
4. **Disclaimer wording.** Something like: "Shadow Dungeon is a free, non-commercial fan project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed or sponsored by Nintendo, Creatures Inc., GAME FREAK inc. or The Pokémon Company. Pokémon and related names are trademarks of their respective owners. Sprites: PMDCollab SpriteCollab contributors (CC BY-NC 4.0). Original code and audio © the author." Do not claim "fair use". It is a US defence, it is decided case by case, and Spanish/EU law has no general fair-use clause [Inference]. Claiming it invites argument rather than goodwill.
5. **Keep a low press profile.** Do not pitch it to gaming press. Frame it as "a little game for a stream community". Avoid launching near major Pokémon releases or Pokémon Presents events.
6. **Hosting.** The own-domain static site is fine. Note that whoever serves the bytes (the CDN or host behind fullfran.com, plus GitHub for the repo) receives the DMCA. Mirroring to itch.io adds another notice target and itch.io page ads are off by default for free games [Unverified], so it gives no clear benefit. Consider keeping the **built game assets out of the public repo**, or making the repo private, if you want fewer notice surfaces. A public repo of engine code plus a CC BY-NC sprite manifest is lower-profile than one that bundles all the sprites [Inference].
7. **If a notice arrives (DMCA to a host or C&D to you):**
   - Comply promptly and politely. Take the game offline, and the repo too if named. Do not counter-notice: a counter-notice is a sworn statement and exposes you to a lawsuit in the US.
   - Do not publicise it, rehost it or encourage mirrors. Ask the streamer not to make it a drama segment.
   - Keep a private copy, the notice text and dates.
   - Treat it as the trigger to execute the original-IP plan (section 7).
   - If the letter demands money or names you personally as a defendant, get a lawyer. That has not happened to any non-commercial fan game found here.
8. **Stream-specific.** Tell the streamer: no official Pokémon music or audio playing over the game (playlists and background tracks), no "Pokémon" in the stream title (use "Shadow Dungeon fan game"), and a clip-friendly but non-hype framing.

### What changes for a bigger single-player story game

- **[Inference]** Risk rises with every "full game" signal: a long story, a world map, a release date, trailers, press, download builds and a Steam-style page. Uranium, Prism and AM2R were all long-development, finished-feeling games hit at release.
- An announced release date is a known exposure point (Prism). Prefer a quiet, gradual browser release over a big launch.
- Any crowdfunding or Patreon for a Pokémon-IP story game is the single clearest red flag in the evidence (McGowan quote).
- More original writing does not reduce character-copyright exposure. Every creature on screen is still a Pokémon design.
- Recommendation: **build the bigger game as original IP from the start** and keep the Pokémon version as the small, free stream toy.

## 6. Path to an original-IP version on the same engine

**[Fact] / settled principles (US, and broadly EU):**

- Game mechanics and rules are not protected by copyright (US 17 U.S.C. §102(b) idea/expression; EU: *SAS Institute v World Programming*, CJEU C-406/10, 2012, holds that functionality is not protected). Turn-based grid movement, type effectiveness charts, recruitment, dungeon floors, hunger and items are free to reuse in principle.
- **Expression is protected.** That covers character designs, sprites, art, music, text, names (as trademarks), logos, and distinctive UI and presentation. US courts have found "look and feel" infringement where a clone copied visual expression closely (*Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive*, D.N.J. 2012; *Spry Fox v. 6waves*, W.D. Wash. 2012) [Fact: case names and holdings as commonly reported. Read the opinions before relying on them].
- **Patents are the exception for mechanics.** Nintendo/TPC's Palworld suit asserts patents on capture and summon mechanics (for example US 12,403,397 and Japanese patents). Patents protect *specific claimed methods*, not genres. The relevant claims (throwing a ball to capture, aiming and summoning a creature into a field battle) are action and real-time mechanics. A turn-based grid dungeon crawler is a different thing [Inference]. Pocketpair responded by patching mechanics, which shows that design-around is feasible.

**Practical plan [Inference]:**

1. **Keep:** the engine, dungeon generation, turn system, AI, the *structure* of the stat and type system (rename the types, rebalance the chart, change the counts), the item and move *systems*, and the procedural audio.
2. **Replace:** every species, sprite, portrait, name, move name, item name, type name and ability name, plus any story beats or terms lifted from PMD (for example "Explorers", "Rescue Team", "Treasure Town"). SpriteCollab art cannot go into an original-IP game because it depicts Pokémon designs. That applies even if the game is non-commercial, and it doubly applies if the game is commercial, since CC BY-NC forbids that.
3. **Avoid trade dress:** no PMD-like portrait-box layout copied pixel for pixel, no red-white ball iconography, no recognisable silhouettes or recolours of Pokémon, and no "Mystery Dungeon" phrase. "Mystery Dungeon" is a Spike Chunsoft series name and possibly a registered mark [Unverified].
4. **Architecture tip:** isolate all IP-bearing data (species tables, names, sprite manifest, text) behind a content-pack boundary. Then the original-IP game is a content swap, and a takedown of the fan pack does not touch the engine.
5. **Before going commercial**, run a quick patent and trademark search for the final name, and get a short professional review. The Palworld case shows Nintendo will litigate against commercial creature games.
